Topic: Scope creep: how to say no politely
Type: practical tip to do this week (general, no invented client story)

Scope creep rarely starts with a big ask. It starts with "can you just add one more thing?"

I see this a lot, on both sides of the table. The client is not being difficult. They just had a new idea, and asking costs them nothing. The agency says yes because it wants to be helpful. Then ten small yeses later, the project is late and everyone is a little annoyed.

Saying no does not have to sound like no. Try this: "Yes, we can do that. It sits outside what we agreed, so here is what it would add in time and cost. Want us to include it, or save it for the next phase?"

You have not refused anything. You have just made the trade visible.

This week, write the scope of your current project in five lines and share it. Next time a new idea comes up, point back to it.

What is the smallest "one more thing" that ever turned into a big one for you?
