Topic: Why the first brief matters more than the first draft
Type: lesson from running an agency (general, no invented client story)

Most creative work does not go wrong in the design. It goes wrong in the brief.

I see this a lot. A business owner sends a short message like "we need a new brochure, something modern", and the first draft comes back. It looks fine. It is also not what they pictured, because nobody ever said what the picture was.

Then come the rounds of changes. Everyone gets tired, and the work ends up as a compromise.

A good brief does not need to be long. It needs four answers: who is this for, what should they do after seeing it, what is one thing it must say, and what does it absolutely not need to do.

Spend thirty minutes on those four lines before anyone opens a design tool. It will save you days later.

What is the vaguest brief you have ever sent, and how did the first draft turn out?
